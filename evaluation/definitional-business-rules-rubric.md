# Definitional Business Rules Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a set of definitional, or structural, business rules is well-formed. Definitional business rules indicate that something is necessarily true or untrue; they are definitional criteria for concepts and information. Used by the business-analysis and quality skills. Based on BABOK Business Rules Analysis (10.9).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Atomic | Each rule states a single definitional criterion. |
| 2 | Definitional | Each rule defines what is necessarily true, not conduct to be enforced. |
| 3 | Consistent | The rules are consistent with the concept model and with each other. |
| 4 | Sourced | Each rule traces to a policy, standard, or authority. |
| 5 | Non-conflicting | The rules do not contradict each other. |
| 6 | Unambiguous | Each rule is stated so it can be applied consistently. |
| 7 | Owned | Each rule has an owner who can change it. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 21):

- Pass: 17 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 12 to 16, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 12, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the business-analysis skill.
