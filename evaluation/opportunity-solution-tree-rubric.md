# Opportunity Solution Tree Evaluation Rubric

Assess an Opportunity Solution Tree against `templates/opportunity-solution-tree.md`, the BABOK Agile Perspective (11.1), and Define Future State (6.2). The tree is judged on whether every solution traces up through an evidence-based opportunity to one measurable outcome, and whether the riskiest assumptions are being tested before commitment. Used by the product-manager skill.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent or misleading, 1 weak, 2 adequate for the stated stage, 3 strong and well supported.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Single measurable outcome | The root is one outcome with a metric, baseline, and target, not a feature or an output. |
| 2 | Evidence-based opportunities | Each opportunity is a customer need, pain, or desire with a cited discovery source, not internal opinion. |
| 3 | Opportunity breadth | Several distinct opportunities are explored before converging, and they are sized or ranked by importance. |
| 4 | Solution trace | Every solution addresses exactly one named opportunity, and no solution floats without an opportunity above it. |
| 5 | Solution alternatives | Priority opportunities have more than one candidate solution, so the team is comparing options rather than validating a favourite. |
| 6 | Assumptions made explicit | Each solution lists the value, usability, feasibility, and viability assumptions it depends on. |
| 7 | Riskiest-first experiments | The riskiest assumptions have the smallest experiment that could disprove them, with a type and a planned or actual result. |
| 8 | Learning loop | Experiment results update the tree, and disproved solutions or opportunities are marked rather than silently kept. |

## Scoring anchors

Anchors describe the extreme scores; 1 and 2 interpolate between them.

| # | Score 0 (absent) | Score 3 (strong) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The root is a feature or an output, or there are several roots. | The root is one outcome with a metric, baseline, and target, not a feature or an output. |
| 2 | Opportunities are internal opinions or disguised solutions. | Each opportunity is a customer need, pain, or desire with a cited discovery source, not internal opinion. |
| 3 | One opportunity explored; the team jumped straight to a solution. | Several distinct opportunities are explored before converging, and they are sized or ranked by importance. |
| 4 | Solutions float with no opportunity above them. | Every solution addresses exactly one named opportunity, and no solution floats without an opportunity above it. |
| 5 | Each opportunity has exactly one solution, the favourite. | Priority opportunities have more than one candidate solution, so the team is comparing options rather than validating a favourite. |
| 6 | Solutions carry no stated assumptions. | Each solution lists the value, usability, feasibility, and viability assumptions it depends on. |
| 7 | No experiments, or experiments that cannot disprove anything. | The riskiest assumptions have the smallest experiment that could disprove them, with a type and a planned or actual result. |
| 8 | Results are never fed back; disproved ideas stay on the tree. | Experiment results update the tree, and disproved solutions or opportunities are marked rather than silently kept. |

## Common failure modes

- Opportunities written as "we need a portal".
- A tree drawn once for a roadmap review and never updated.
- Experiments chosen for ease rather than for the riskiest assumption.
- Outcome set as a vanity metric such as page views.

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or 20 or higher with a dimension at 0.
- Fail: below 14.

Dimensions 1 (single measurable outcome) and 4 (solution trace) are blocking: a score of 0 on either fails the document whatever the total. Record findings by severity with specific fixes, and route material issues back to the product-manager skill. A draft that does not pass may still be useful; its quality state must remain visible.
