# Affinity Diagram Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether an affinity diagram groups items into meaningful themes. An affinity diagram organizes a large set of ideas or findings into related groups. Used by the elicitation and visual-modelling skills. Based on BABOK Collaborative Games (10.10) and Brainstorming (10.5).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Items captured | The items grouped are the ones the activity produced, with none lost. |
| 2 | Grouping valid | Items are grouped by genuine affinity, not superficial similarity. |
| 3 | Themes labelled | Each group carries a label that names the theme. |
| 4 | No orphans | Every item belongs to a group, or a residual group is named for the rest. |
| 5 | Mutually distinct | The groups are distinct, with minimal overlap. |
| 6 | Coverage | The set of themes covers the material without a major gap. |
| 7 | Actionable | The grouping supports the next step, such as prioritization or analysis. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 21):

- Pass: 17 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 12 to 16, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 12, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the elicitation skill.
