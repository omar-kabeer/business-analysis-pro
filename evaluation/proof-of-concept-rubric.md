# Proof of Concept Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a proof of concept answers its feasibility question. A proof of concept is a limited build that tests whether an approach is feasible before committing to it. Used by the prototyping and architecture skills. Based on BABOK Prototyping (10.36).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Question defined | The feasibility question the proof of concept answers is stated. |
| 2 | Scope focused | The build is limited to what is needed to answer the question. |
| 3 | Evidence | The result provides evidence for or against feasibility. |
| 4 | Conclusion | A clear conclusion is drawn from the result. |
| 5 | Assumptions | The assumptions and conditions of the test are stated. |
| 6 | Limits | What the proof of concept does not prove is stated, to avoid over-reading it. |
| 7 | Next step | The decision the result informs is identified. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 21):

- Pass: 17 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 12 to 16, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 12, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the prototyping skill.
