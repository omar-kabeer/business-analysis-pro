# Product Brief Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a product idea or product brief is clear and grounded enough to take forward into a PRD or a business case. It is used by the quality skill and applied to any product concept, product brief, or opportunity pitch. Score each dimension, note evidence and gaps, then give a verdict.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3:

- 0 Absent: not present or unusable.
- 1 Weak: present but with material gaps.
- 2 Adequate: meets the bar with minor gaps.
- 3 Strong: complete and high quality.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Problem and need | The customer problem the product solves is explicit, evidenced, and framed as a need rather than a feature. |
| 2 | Target users | The target users and buyers are named, with their context and the job they are trying to get done. |
| 3 | Value proposition | The core value and why it is compelling is stated, with how it differs from existing alternatives. |
| 4 | Market and competition | The market and the main alternatives or competitors are described, with where this product fits. |
| 5 | Solution outline | The proposed solution and its main capabilities are outlined at the right altitude, without over-specifying. |
| 6 | Success measures | The outcomes the product must achieve are stated with measurable success metrics and targets. |
| 7 | Scope and phasing | An initial scope, a minimum viable slice, and what is deferred are drawn. |
| 8 | Assumptions and risks | Key assumptions, dependencies, and risks are explicit, with their impact if wrong. |
| 9 | Next step | A clear recommendation on the next deliverable, such as a PRD or business case, or an explicit stopping point. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 27). Record a verdict:

- Pass: 22 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 16 to 21, or any single dimension at 1 that is quick to fix.
- Fail: below 16, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material gaps back to the product-manager or strategy skill.
