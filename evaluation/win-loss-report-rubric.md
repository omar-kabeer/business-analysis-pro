# Win/Loss Report Evaluation Rubric

Assess a Win/Loss Report against `templates/win-loss-report.md`, BABOK Benchmarking and Market Analysis (10.4), and Interviews (10.25). The report is judged on whether its findings come from buyers on both sides and whether each recommendation traces to evidence. Used by the product-manager skill, with market inputs from market-research.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent or misleading, 1 weak, 2 adequate for the stated stage, 3 strong and well supported.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Method and sample | Deal selection, the number of won and lost buyers interviewed, the period, and the questions used are stated. |
| 2 | Balanced sample | Both won and lost deals are interviewed in meaningful numbers, and any skew or selection bias is acknowledged. |
| 3 | Evidence behind themes | Each win and loss theme is supported by counts or quotes, not by impressions or internal belief. |
| 4 | Factor separation | Product, pricing and packaging, messaging and positioning, and sales process factors are analysed separately. |
| 5 | Competitive insight | Competitor strengths and gaps are specific and attributed to buyer evidence. |
| 6 | Implications | Each theme states what it means for the product, pricing, messaging, or sales approach. |
| 7 | Traceable recommendations | Recommendations are prioritised, owned, and each tied to a named finding. |
| 8 | Honesty about limits | Sample size, confidence, and open questions are stated, and conclusions are not stretched beyond the evidence. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or 20 or higher with a dimension at 0.
- Fail: below 14.

Record findings by severity with specific fixes. Route material issues back to the product-manager skill for revision. An artefact that does not pass may still be useful as a draft; its quality state must remain visible.
