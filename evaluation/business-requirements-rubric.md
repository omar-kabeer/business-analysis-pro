# Business Requirements Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether the business requirements frame the change soundly. Business requirements are the problem, opportunity, or constraint of strategic importance that drives the change, defined from an understanding of the current state and expressed from the enterprise perspective. Used by the strategy and business-analysis skills. Based on BABOK Analyze Current State (6.1).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Enterprise perspective | The requirement is stated from the enterprise view, not one team's. |
| 2 | Problem, opportunity, or constraint | It is framed clearly as a problem, an opportunity, or a constraint, not a solution. |
| 3 | Grounded in current state | It is defined from an understanding of the current state. |
| 4 | Strategic importance | Why it matters, and why now, is clear. |
| 5 | Value | It is tied to a value the enterprise cares about, such as revenue, cost, risk, or experience. |
| 6 | Measurable | The impact or need is quantified where possible. |
| 7 | Solution-free | No solution is presumed in the statement. |
| 8 | Owned | A sponsor or owner is accountable for it. |

## Scoring anchors

Anchors describe the extreme scores; 1 and 2 interpolate between them.

| # | Score 0 (absent) | Score 3 (strong) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Written from one team's view. | Stated from the enterprise perspective. |
| 2 | Framed as a solution or a feature. | Framed as a problem, opportunity, or constraint. |
| 3 | Floats free of the current state. | Defined from an understanding of the current state. |
| 4 | No sense of importance or timing. | Why it matters and why now is clear. |
| 5 | No link to enterprise value. | Tied to revenue, cost, risk, or experience. |
| 6 | Impact unquantified. | Impact or need quantified where possible. |
| 7 | A solution is baked in. | Solution-free. |
| 8 | Unowned. | A sponsor or owner is accountable. |

## Common failure modes

- Solution as requirement: "we need a new CRM" instead of the problem the CRM would solve.
- Team-level framing: a departmental gripe presented as an enterprise business requirement.
- Unquantified need: "improve customer satisfaction" with no measure of the current shortfall.
- No current-state grounding: the requirement asserts a problem the current state description does not evidence.
- Orphaned importance: a problem stated with no reason it is strategic or why it must be addressed now.
- No owner: a business requirement no sponsor stands behind, so it cannot be prioritized against others.

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 14, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the strategy skill.
