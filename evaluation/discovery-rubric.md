# Discovery Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a discovery output frames a problem well enough to move into requirements or a business case. It is used by the quality skill and applied to any discovery brief, problem statement, or opportunity assessment. Score each dimension, note evidence and gaps, then give a verdict.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3:

- 0 Absent: not present or unusable.
- 1 Weak: present but with material gaps.
- 2 Adequate: meets the bar with minor gaps.
- 3 Strong: complete and high quality.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Problem framing | The business problem or opportunity is stated as an outcome, not a solution, and it is clear why it matters now. |
| 2 | Business need and value | The underlying need is explicit and tied to a value the organisation cares about, such as revenue, cost, risk, or experience. |
| 3 | Stakeholders | The affected stakeholders and users are identified with their interests and the decisions they own. |
| 4 | Current state | The current situation, process, or system is described with enough evidence to be trusted, not assumed. |
| 5 | Desired outcome | The future outcome and what success looks like are described, with early measures of success. |
| 6 | Scope and boundaries | What is in and out of scope is drawn, so the work does not sprawl. |
| 7 | Assumptions and unknowns | Assumptions, open questions, and the information still missing are listed rather than hidden. |
| 8 | Constraints and risks | Constraints such as time, budget, compliance, and systems are captured, with the main risks and their impact. |
| 9 | Next step | A clear recommendation on the next step and the deliverable it should produce, or an explicit stopping point. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 27). Record a verdict:

- Pass: 22 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 16 to 21, or any single dimension at 1 that is quick to fix.
- Fail: below 16, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material gaps back to the elicitation or business-analysis skill.
