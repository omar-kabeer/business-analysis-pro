# Requirements and Delivery Frameworks

Frameworks for turning needs into buildable, verifiable work. Used by the requirements, product-owner, and quality skills and the `user-story-epic`, `requirements-traceability-matrix`, and checklist assets.

## INVEST

- Purpose: judge the quality of a user story.
- When to use: writing or refining backlog items.
- Method: a good story is Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, and Testable. If a story fails one, reshape or split it.
- Pitfalls: stories that are tasks; acceptance criteria missing so the story is not testable.

## Definition of Ready and Definition of Done

- Purpose: gate work into and out of a sprint.
- When to use: any cadenced delivery.
- Method: Ready means the item is clear, sized, has acceptance criteria, and has no blocking unknowns. Done means built, reviewed, tested, non-functional needs checked, documented, and accepted. See the `definition-of-ready` and `definition-of-done` checklists.
- Pitfalls: gates so heavy nothing passes, or so light that unclear work starts and unfinished work is called done.

## Traceability

- Purpose: keep requirements auditable and change-safe.
- When to use: any initiative where scope and change must be controlled.
- Method: maintain links from business need to requirement to design to test, in both directions. See the `requirements-traceability-matrix` template.
- Pitfalls: a matrix that is created once and never updated; orphan requirements with no source or test.
