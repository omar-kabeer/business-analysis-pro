# Strategy Frameworks

Frameworks for analysing position and shaping direction. Used by the strategy, market-research, and business-analysis skills and the `swot-analysis`, `business-model-canvas`, and `future-state-definition` templates. Each is only useful if it changes a decision.

## PESTLE

- Purpose: scan the macro environment.
- When to use: before a market entry or a major bet.
- Method: assess Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental forces, and flag which accelerate or threaten the change.
- Pitfalls: a list with no implication; treating every factor as equally relevant.

## Porter's Five Forces

- Purpose: judge the structural attractiveness of a market.
- When to use: assessing where profit pools sit and how defensible a position is.
- Method: assess rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, buyer power, and supplier power. Higher forces mean thinner margins.
- Pitfalls: static snapshot; ignoring how forces shift over time.

## SWOT and TOWS

- Purpose: assess position and generate strategy.
- When to use: a rounded internal and external view that must lead to action.
- Method: strengths and weaknesses (internal), opportunities and threats (external), each evidence-based. Use the TOWS matrix to generate strategies: use strengths to seize opportunities (SO) or counter threats (ST), and fix weaknesses to seize opportunities (WO) or avoid threats (WT).
- Pitfalls: a generic grid with no strategies; opinion instead of evidence.

## Business Model Canvas

- Purpose: describe how an enterprise creates, delivers, and captures value.
- When to use: understanding or redesigning a business model.
- Method: complete the nine blocks (customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure) and check they fit together.
- Pitfalls: blocks that do not connect; revenue that does not exceed cost.

## Value Proposition Canvas

- Purpose: sharpen the fit between what customers need and what you offer.
- When to use: refining product-market fit.
- Method: match customer jobs, pains, and gains to your pain relievers and gain creators.
- Pitfalls: claiming relievers for pains no one has.

## OKRs

- Purpose: make strategy executable and trackable.
- When to use: turning direction into measurable near-term focus.
- Method: a qualitative Objective plus 3 to 5 measurable Key Results. Keep the set small.
- Pitfalls: key results that are tasks, not outcomes; too many objectives.

## Balanced Scorecard

- Purpose: a rounded view of performance.
- When to use: measuring beyond financials.
- Method: track measures across financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth perspectives.
- Pitfalls: too many measures; no link between perspectives and strategy.
