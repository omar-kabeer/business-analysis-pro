# Prioritisation Frameworks

Pick one method per decision and apply it consistently. Tie the result back to the outcome you are trying to move. Used by the product-manager, product-owner, and governance skills and the `prioritization-matrix` template.

## RICE

- Purpose: compare many items on expected value per unit of effort.
- When to use: a backlog or opportunity list where you can estimate reach and impact.
- Method: score Reach times Impact times Confidence, divided by Effort. State the scales (for example reach as users per quarter, impact 0.5 to 3, confidence as a percentage, effort in person-weeks).
- Pitfalls: false precision from guessed inputs; padding confidence to justify a favourite.

## MoSCoW

- Purpose: agree scope for a release.
- When to use: negotiating what is in and out with stakeholders.
- Method: classify each item as Must, Should, Could, or Won't now. Keep Musts to what is genuinely non-negotiable.
- Pitfalls: everything becomes a Must; no agreement on what Won't now means.

## Kano

- Purpose: balance basic, performance, and delight needs.
- When to use: shaping a product or feature set for satisfaction.
- Method: classify features as basic (expected, dissatisfy if absent), performance (more is better), or delight (unexpected upside). Cover basics before investing in delight.
- Pitfalls: chasing delighters while basics fail; treating all features as equal.

## WSJF

- Purpose: sequence work by economic value and flow.
- When to use: scaled or flow-based delivery.
- Method: Weighted Shortest Job First equals cost of delay divided by job size. Do the highest ratio first.
- Pitfalls: cost of delay is hard to estimate; treat it as relative, not absolute.

## Value vs Effort

- Purpose: fast, coarse triage.
- When to use: quick first pass before a finer method.
- Method: plot items on a 2 by 2 of value and effort. Do high value and low effort first; avoid low value and high effort.
- Pitfalls: too coarse to separate close calls; hides dependencies.
