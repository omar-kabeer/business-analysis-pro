# Business Capability Map

## Purpose

Set out what the business is able to do, independent of who does it or how, and rate each capability so that investment can be aimed at the places that matter.

Build the motivation first. Without it the map is inventory.

## Capability hierarchy

| Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 | Definition | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rules: capabilities are nouns describing what, never how; they never carry a department or system name; siblings do not overlap; level 1 holds ten to fifteen at most.

## Assessment

| Capability | Current performance (1-5) | Strategic importance (1-5) | Quadrant | Evidence | Investment implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Quadrant: high importance and low performance is where investment belongs. High performance and low importance is a candidate for reduction.

## Value stream cross-map

| Value stream | Stage | Capabilities used | Pain at this stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

A capability appearing across many streams is a shared asset and a single point of failure. Mark it.

## Transition states

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Every transition state must be a place the organisation could stand indefinitely. If it is not, it is not a transition state.
