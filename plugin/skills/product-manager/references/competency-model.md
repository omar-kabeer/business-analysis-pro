# The 12-Competency Model

A standard for assessing and developing product managers, organised into four themes with three competencies each (as used by Reforge and industry product leaders). Use it to judge a PM's strengths, plan development, and calibrate the depth expected at each level.

## Theme 1: Product Execution

Turning ideas into shipped, valuable product.

- Feature specification: gather complete requirements, articulate edge cases, define acceptance criteria, and write clear, unambiguous specifications.
- Product delivery: work daily with the cross-functional pod to execute sprints, unblock dependencies, contain scope creep, and ship to production at a healthy cadence.
- Product quality: identify, prioritise, and resolve defects, performance issues, technical debt, and usability flaws that erode value or reputation.

## Theme 2: Customer Insight

Making sure what is built matches verified demand and real behaviour.

- Fluency with data: set baselines, track performance, form testable hypotheses, interpret behavioural telemetry, and connect user actions to business results.
- Voice of the customer: design, run, and synthesise continuous research and feedback into insights about unmet needs and anxieties.
- User experience design: evaluate and co-create intuitive flows, information architecture, and interactions with design to reduce friction.

## Theme 3: Product Strategy

Elevating from feature delivery to enterprise value.

- Business outcome ownership: be accountable for connecting features to revenue, CAC reduction, retention, LTV, or operational efficiency.
- Product vision and roadmapping: articulate an inspiring long-term destination and sequence milestones into an adaptable, prioritised roadmap.
- Strategic impact: read industry shifts, competitive moats, market sizing, and portfolio synergies, and use them to influence direction.

## Theme 4: Influencing People

Driving alignment without direct authority.

- Stakeholder management: navigate sales, legal, marketing, support, and compliance to resolve competing priorities and secure buy-in.
- Team leadership: build psychological safety, inspire the squad without line authority, and hold accountability to a shared mission.
- Managing up: engage executives and sponsors to remove systemic blockers, refine strategy, and secure funding.

## Career levels

The same competencies apply at every level, but the weight shifts. Junior PMs lean on product execution and customer insight for a single feature or squad. Senior and principal PMs lean on product strategy and influencing people across a product line or portfolio, owning larger outcomes and shaping organisational direction.
