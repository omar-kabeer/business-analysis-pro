# Product Manager Knowledge Base

The standing knowledge a strong PM draws on, beyond frameworks.

## Business acumen and unit economics

Understand cash flow, unit economics, and margin. Know the common pricing models: freemium, tiered SaaS, and usage-based. Judge a feature not only on whether it can be built but on whether it improves defensibility, expands margin, or lifts retention. Core metrics: customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), gross margin, payback period, and the LTV to CAC ratio.

## Technical literacy

Enough to earn engineering's trust and judge feasibility: web and mobile architecture, monolith versus microservices, API styles (REST, GraphQL), relational and non-relational databases, data schema and serialisation (JSON, XML), authentication, and webhooks. Understand technical debt trade-offs and advocate for platform health, not just features.

## Quantitative and qualitative methods

- Quantitative: define experiment metrics, query data (SQL), run A/B and multivariate tests, and interpret event analytics.
- Qualitative: structured customer interviews, usability testing, and observation to surface latent, unstated needs.

Use both. Quantitative-only optimises local maxima and misses market shifts; qualitative-only over-fits to a vocal minority.

## Key performance indicators

Activation, retention (cohort), conversion, engagement (monthly and daily active users), net promoter score, churn, expansion revenue, CAC, LTV, and gross margin. Choose the few that reflect the outcome you own.

## Usability and behavioural science

Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics for interface design, and common cognitive biases that shape user behaviour: default bias, framing effect, loss aversion, and choice overload. Design with these in mind and do not exploit them against the user's interest.

## Bodies of knowledge

- SVPG (Marty Cagan): empowered product teams, product discovery, and the four product risks (value, usability, feasibility, viability).
- Pragmatic Framework: the market-driven, 37-box product lifecycle.
- Reforge: growth, retention, monetisation, and the competency model.
- AIPMM ProdBOK: standard definitions, processes, and terminology.

## Regulatory and compliance awareness

Know the regimes that constrain product decisions: GDPR and CCPA (privacy), HIPAA (health), SOC 2 (security controls), and any sector rules. Treat them as constraints and non-functional requirements, and pull the relevant domain pack when one applies.

## Product operations

As a product org grows past roughly ten to fifteen PMs, product operations takes on tooling, analytics, feedback pipelines, and planning rituals, freeing PMs to focus on discovery and strategy. Know when to ask for it.

## AI-augmented practice

Use AI to synthesise large volumes of qualitative input (transcripts, tickets, reviews) into insight clusters, draft baseline PRDs, generate edge cases and experiment variants, and write release notes. Keep human judgement for vision, ethical trade-offs, stakeholder navigation, and deep empathy.
