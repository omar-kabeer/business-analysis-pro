# Product Discovery and Strategy Reference

Grounded in modern product management practice (Marty Cagan and SVPG, Teresa Torres, Melissa Perri, Roman Pichler).

## Outcome over output

Lead with problems and measurable results, not feature lists. Define a north-star metric that captures the value customers get, plus a small set of input signals that lead it.

## Jobs to be Done

People "hire" a product to make progress in a situation. Capture the job, the circumstances, and the desired progress. Frame with: when [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].

## Opportunity Solution Tree

- Desired outcome at the root.
- Opportunities (unmet needs, pains, desires) as branches, sourced from discovery.
- Solutions under the opportunities they address.
- Experiments under solutions to test assumptions.

The tree keeps solutions tied to a real opportunity and to the outcome.

## Continuous discovery

Small, frequent research: weekly customer touchpoints, assumption mapping, and quick tests (interview, prototype, fake door, concierge). De-risk the biggest unknowns first: value, usability, feasibility, viability.

## Prioritisation

- RICE: Reach times Impact times Confidence divided by Effort.
- Value vs Effort: quick 2x2 for coarse triage.
- Kano: basic, performance, and delight needs; do not over-invest in delight while basics fail.

Tie whatever method you use back to the north-star outcome.

## Roadmap

Prefer now-next-later over dated Gantt promises. Communicate outcomes and confidence levels. Revisit as discovery changes the evidence.

## Go-to-market and monetisation

Positioning (who it is for and why it is different), pricing model (value-based where possible), packaging, and launch plan. Align these with the value proposition, not with cost-plus habit.
