# UX Artifacts Reference

Structures for the core UX artifacts a business analyst or product role produces, grounded in evidence.

## Persona

A believable, evidence-based archetype of a user segment. Capture: name and role, context and goals, tasks, frustrations and pains, needs, and the environment and devices used. Base it on research; label anything assumed. Avoid inventing demographic detail that does not affect design.

## Empathy map

For the target persona, capture what they say, think, do, and feel, plus their pains and gains. A fast way to build shared understanding of a user before designing.

## Journey map

Map the user's experience across stages of a task or relationship. For each stage capture: the user's actions, their thoughts, their emotions (the highs and lows), pain points, and opportunities. A current-state journey exposes where the experience breaks; a future-state journey shows the target.

## Service blueprint

Extends the journey to the back stage. Layers: customer actions, front-stage interactions (what the user sees and touches), back-stage actions (staff and systems the user does not see), and supporting processes. Use when front-stage and back-stage coordination matters.

## Usability heuristics (Nielsen)

Review an interface against recognised heuristics: visibility of system status, match to the real world, user control and freedom, consistency and standards, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility and efficiency, aesthetic and minimalist design, help users recover from errors, and help and documentation. Flag each issue with the heuristic it breaks and a specific fix.

## Accessibility (WCAG 2.1)

Assess against the four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable, robust. Target level AA for most products. Check colour contrast, keyboard operability, focus order, alternative text, labels and instructions, and error identification. Call out specific failures and fixes, not a vague pass or fail.

## Information architecture and wireframes

Describe the structure and navigation (how content and functions are organised and found), the hierarchy on key screens, and the intent of each screen or state, expressed so requirements and design can execute. Keep wireframe guidance at the level of layout and intent, not visual styling.
