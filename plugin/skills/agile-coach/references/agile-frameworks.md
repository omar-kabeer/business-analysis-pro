# Agile Frameworks and Practices

## Choosing an approach

- Scrum: fixed-length sprints, a sprint goal, and defined roles. Good when work benefits from a regular cadence and a committed goal.
- Kanban: visualise the workflow, limit work in progress, and manage flow. Good for continuous or unpredictable work (support, operations, a steady request stream).
- XP practices: pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration, refactoring. Engineering health practices that complement Scrum or Kanban.
- Scaling (SAFe, LeSS): only when several teams must coordinate on one product. Adds structure and overhead; do not scale a problem you can solve with one team.

## Scrum in brief

- Roles: product owner (what and why), scrum master (process and impediments), developers (how).
- Events: sprint, sprint planning, daily scrum, sprint review, sprint retrospective, plus ongoing backlog refinement.
- Artifacts: product backlog, sprint backlog, increment, with a definition of done.

## Kanban in brief

- Visualise the workflow on a board with explicit columns.
- Limit work in progress per column to expose bottlenecks.
- Manage flow: measure cycle time and throughput, and reduce blockers.
- Make policies explicit and improve collaboratively.

## Ceremonies (purpose and timebox)

- Sprint planning: agree the sprint goal and the work to meet it.
- Daily standup: surface progress and blockers, not a status report to a manager. Keep it short.
- Review or demo: show working output to stakeholders and gather feedback.
- Retrospective: inspect how the team worked and agree improvements. The engine of continuous improvement.
- Refinement: prepare upcoming backlog items so they meet the definition of ready.

## Flow and WIP

Lower work in progress shortens cycle time and surfaces bottlenecks. When everything is in progress, nothing finishes. Make blockers visible and swarm on them.

## Metrics

- Velocity: the team's own planning aid, not a productivity target and never a cross-team comparison.
- Cycle time and lead time: how long work takes; shorter and more predictable is better.
- Throughput: items completed per period.
Use metrics to learn and forecast, not to pressure. Metrics used as targets get gamed.

## Retrospective formats

Rotate formats to keep them fresh: what went well and what to improve; start, stop, continue; the sailboat (wind and anchors); or a timeline of the sprint. End with a small number of concrete, owned actions.
