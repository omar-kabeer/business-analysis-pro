# Market Sizing and Scanning Reference

## TAM, SAM, SOM

- TAM (Total Addressable Market): total demand if you captured 100 percent of the defined market.
- SAM (Serviceable Available Market): the portion your model and geography can actually serve.
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): the realistic near-term share given competition and capacity.

## Two methods, always reconcile

- Top-down: start from an industry figure and narrow by segment, geography, and qualification. Fast but inherits the source's error.
- Bottom-up: units times price, built from customer counts or usage. Example: (target accounts) x (average contract value) x (realistic penetration). More defensible; prefer it as the anchor.

Reconcile the two. A large gap signals a bad assumption somewhere.

## Sourcing discipline

Every figure carries a source or a stated assumption. Prefer primary and recent data. Note data quality and recency. Separate observed fact from estimate.

## Competitive mapping

- Direct competitors: same problem, same approach.
- Indirect competitors: same problem, different approach.
- Substitutes: the "do nothing", spreadsheet, or manual workaround.
- Basis of competition: price, features, service, distribution, brand, switching costs.

## Attractiveness: Five Forces

Rivalry intensity, threat of entry, threat of substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. Higher forces mean thinner profit pools.

## Trend scan: PESTLE

Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental drivers. Flag which ones accelerate demand and which threaten it, over the stated horizon.

## Segmentation and beachhead

Segment by need, behaviour, firmographic, or demographic. Pick a beachhead where the pain is sharp, the segment is reachable, and you can win before expanding.
