# Product Strategy Evaluation Rubric

Assess a Product Strategy Document against `templates/product-strategy.md`, BABOK Define Future State (6.2), Business Model Canvas (10.8), and the Agile Perspective (11.1). A product strategy is judged on whether it explains how the product wins and constrains the roadmap, rather than listing features. Used by the product-manager skill.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent or misleading, 1 weak, 2 adequate for the stated stage, 3 strong and well supported.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Vision link and north star | The strategy names the vision it serves and a single north-star outcome with a baseline and target. |
| 2 | Segment focus | Target segments are prioritised, each tied to a job to be done and a reason it matters, and the priority order is justified. |
| 3 | Differentiated positioning | Positioning names who the product is for and why it is different from the primary alternatives, specifically enough to be disagreed with. |
| 4 | Value proposition evidence | Core value propositions rest on customer or market evidence, and the evidence is cited rather than assumed. |
| 5 | Revenue model and unit economics | The pricing model, packaging, and the unit economics that matter are stated, with the assumptions behind them. |
| 6 | Strategic bets | Two to four bets are stated, each tested for customer delight, a named defensible advantage, and its margin effect. |
| 7 | Deliberate exclusions | The strategy states where the product will not play, and the exclusions are real choices, not omissions. |
| 8 | Roadmap linkage | The bets and exclusions give the roadmap clear direction, so a proposed roadmap item could be judged in or out of strategy. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or 20 or higher with a dimension at 0.
- Fail: below 14.

Record findings by severity with specific fixes. Route material issues back to the product-manager skill for revision. An artefact that does not pass may still be useful as a draft; its quality state must remain visible.
