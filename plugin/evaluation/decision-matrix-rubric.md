# Decision Matrix Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a decision matrix supports a sound choice. A decision matrix, simple or weighted, scores options against criteria to compare them. Used by the decision-analysis and visual-modelling skills. Based on BABOK Decision Analysis (10.16).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Options complete | The options being compared are complete and distinct. |
| 2 | Criteria | The criteria are relevant, complete, and independent of each other. |
| 3 | Weighting | Where weighted, the weights are justified and reflect stakeholder priorities. |
| 4 | Scoring consistent | Each option is scored against each criterion on a consistent scale. |
| 5 | Arithmetic correct | The weighted scores and totals are calculated correctly. |
| 6 | Evidence | The scores are backed by evidence or stated judgment, not guesswork. |
| 7 | Conclusion follows | The recommended option follows from the scores. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 21):

- Pass: 17 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 12 to 16, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 12, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the decision-analysis skill.
