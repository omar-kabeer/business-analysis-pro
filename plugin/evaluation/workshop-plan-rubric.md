# Workshop Plan Evaluation Rubric

Assess the whole Workshop Plan (plan, capture, and confirmation) against `templates/workshop-plan.md` and the BABOK Workshops technique (10.50). A planned agenda is a plan, not a result; only decisions, actions, and items actually captured during the session count as observed.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent or misleading, 1 weak, 2 adequate for the stated stage, 3 strong and well supported.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Outcomes and purpose | The desired outcomes and purpose are stated up front, so success can be judged against them. |
| 2 | Participant selection | Participants and roles (sponsor, facilitator, note taker, timekeeper) were selected to cover the knowledge and decision authority the outcomes need. |
| 3 | Preparation and logistics | Pre-work, materials, and the agenda were prepared and shared in advance of the session. |
| 4 | Agenda structure | Each agenda item has a timebox, a named technique or activity, and a stated intended output. |
| 5 | Ground rules and facilitation | Ground rules were agreed, the facilitator stayed neutral on content, and a parking lot held items that would have derailed the agenda. |
| 6 | Capture of decisions and actions | Decisions, actions, and parking lot items are recorded with an owner and, for decisions, a rationale. |
| 7 | Confirmation after the workshop | Notes were distributed and decisions and actions confirmed with participants, matching the Confirm Elicitation Results step (4.3). |
| 8 | Evidence honesty | The record reflects what the group actually decided and raised, not what the agenda planned to cover; unresolved items are shown as open rather than as decisions. |

## Scoring anchors

Anchors describe the extreme scores; 1 and 2 interpolate between them.

| # | Score 0 (absent) | Score 3 (strong) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No outcomes; the session has no way to judge success. | The desired outcomes and purpose are stated up front, so success can be judged against them. |
| 2 | Participants lack the knowledge or decision authority needed. | Participants and roles (sponsor, facilitator, note taker, timekeeper) were selected to cover the knowledge and decision authority the outcomes need. |
| 3 | Nothing prepared or shared in advance. | Pre-work, materials, and the agenda were prepared and shared in advance of the session. |
| 4 | Agenda items have no timebox, technique, or output. | Each agenda item has a timebox, a named technique or activity, and a stated intended output. |
| 5 | No ground rules; tangents take over. | Ground rules were agreed, the facilitator stayed neutral on content, and a parking lot held items that would have derailed the agenda. |
| 6 | Decisions and actions go unrecorded or unowned. | Decisions, actions, and parking lot items are recorded with an owner and, for decisions, a rationale. |
| 7 | Results were never confirmed with participants. | Notes were distributed and decisions and actions confirmed with participants, matching the Confirm Elicitation Results step (4.3). |
| 8 | The record reports the plan as if it happened, or hides what was not settled. | The record reflects what the group actually decided and raised, not what the agenda planned to cover; unresolved items are shown as open rather than as decisions. |

## Common failure modes

- A workshop that is a presentation with questions at the end.
- Decision makers absent, so nothing can be decided.
- Notes that list agenda items rather than outcomes.
- Unfinished items silently dropped.

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or 20 or higher with a dimension at 0.
- Fail: below 14.

Dimensions 1 (outcomes and purpose) and 8 (evidence honesty) are blocking: a score of 0 on either fails the document whatever the total. Record findings by severity with specific fixes, and route material issues back to the elicitation skill. A draft that does not pass may still be useful; its quality state must remain visible.
