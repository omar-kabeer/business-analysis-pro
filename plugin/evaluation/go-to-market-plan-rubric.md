# Go-to-Market Plan Evaluation Rubric

Assess a Go-to-Market Plan against `templates/go-to-market-plan.md`, BABOK Define Change Strategy (6.4), and Benchmarking and Market Analysis (10.4). The plan is judged on whether it turns a build into a launch with evidence behind the positioning, owners behind the readiness, and measurable success. Used by the product-manager skill, with market inputs from market-research and financials from finance.

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent or misleading, 1 weak, 2 adequate for the stated stage, 3 strong and well supported.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Target and positioning | The target segment, its job and pain, and the positioning against primary alternatives are specific and evidence-based. |
| 2 | Messaging | Messages are tailored to each audience and backed by proof points the product can actually support at launch. |
| 3 | Pricing and packaging | The pricing model and tiers have a stated rationale tied to customer value, and launch pricing is time-bound where used. |
| 4 | Channels | Each channel has a defined purpose in awareness, acquisition, or activation and a named owner. |
| 5 | Launch tier and readiness | The launch tier is stated, and readiness across product, sales or success, marketing, support, and legal has owners and a current status. |
| 6 | Timeline | Milestones are dated, owned, and sequenced so dependencies between functions are visible. |
| 7 | Success metrics | Success is defined as measurable metrics with baselines, targets, and a time frame, and each can be observed after launch. |
| 8 | Risks and mitigations | The main launch risks are identified with impact, mitigation, and owner, including the risk that adoption falls short. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 24):

- Pass: 20 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 14 to 19, or 20 or higher with a dimension at 0.
- Fail: below 14.

Record findings by severity with specific fixes. Route material issues back to the product-manager skill for revision. An artefact that does not pass may still be useful as a draft; its quality state must remain visible.
