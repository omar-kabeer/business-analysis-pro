# Concept Model Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether a concept model defines the business vocabulary coherently. A concept model organizes the noun concepts of a business domain and the relationships between them, independent of any data design. Used by the business-analysis and visual-modelling skills. Based on BABOK Concept Modelling (10.11).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Concepts defined | The core concepts of the domain are identified and defined. |
| 2 | Relationships | The relationships between concepts are stated and correct. |
| 3 | Vocabulary | The model uses the language of the business, not of a system. |
| 4 | No ambiguity | Each concept has one agreed meaning, with synonyms and homonyms resolved. |
| 5 | Consistent | The model is internally consistent. |
| 6 | Scope | The model covers the concepts the work requires. |
| 7 | Shared | The vocabulary is agreed by the relevant stakeholders. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 21):

- Pass: 17 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 12 to 16, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 12, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the business-analysis skill.
