# Information Management Approach Evaluation Rubric

A repeatable rubric for judging whether an information management approach is ready to govern how business analysis information is stored, accessed, reused, and maintained. Business analysis information covers requirements, designs, elicitation results, solution options, and more, not requirements alone. Used by the information-management and quality skills and applied to any approach produced from `templates/information-management-approach.md`. Based on BABOK Plan Business Analysis Information Management (3.4).

## Scoring scale

For each dimension score 0 to 3: 0 absent, 1 weak, 2 adequate, 3 strong.

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Scope of information | The approach covers all business analysis information, including elicitation results, designs, and solution options, not just requirements. |
| 2 | Storage and access | Where information is stored, and who can access it under what conditions, is defined. |
| 3 | Traceability | How relationships and traceability between items are captured and maintained is defined. |
| 4 | Reuse | Information is structured, named, and stored so it can be found and reused beyond the current change. |
| 5 | Status and change | The repository indicates the status of each item and supports change over time. |
| 6 | Retention | Retention and archival rules are stated and tied to policy. |
| 7 | Tooling | The tools used and how they shape capture, notation, and organization are stated. |
| 8 | Security and access control | Access rights are appropriate and sensitive information is protected. |
| 9 | Fit and agreement | The approach is consistent with the business analysis and governance approaches and agreed by stakeholders. |

## Result

Total the scores (maximum 27):

- Pass: 22 or higher with no dimension at 0.
- Pass with changes: 16 to 21, or a single quick-to-fix dimension at 1.
- Fail: below 16, or any dimension at 0.

Record findings by severity with a specific fix, and route material issues back to the information-management skill.
