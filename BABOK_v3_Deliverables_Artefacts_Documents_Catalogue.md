# BABOK® v3 — Complete Catalogue of Deliverables, Artefacts & Documents

*A line-by-line extraction from* **A Guide to the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge® (BABOK® Guide), Version 3.0, IIBA (2015).**

This catalogue lists every deliverable, artefact, and document defined or referenced across the guide's 514 pages: the formal task **Outputs** and **Inputs** (the "business analysis information" that flows through the Input/Output model), the **Guidelines & Tools** each task draws on, the **requirements/design** artefact classes, the **packaging & representation** forms, the **named diagram/model/matrix** forms, the **procurement/solicitation documents**, the artefacts produced by all **50 techniques**, and the **perspective-specific** artefacts.

> **Note on method.** BABOK is built on an Input/Output model: every task in Knowledge Areas 3–8 declares its Inputs (section `x.y.3`) and Outputs (section `x.y.8`), and each task diagram lists the *Guidelines and Tools* it uses. An "Output" of one task is frequently an "Input" to another — so the true artefact set is the **deduplicated union** of all inputs and outputs (Section 3). Techniques (Chapter 10) and Perspectives (Chapter 11) introduce further concrete work products (Sections 10–11), while the Glossary and technique *Elements* add packaging, diagram, procurement, and analysis/assessment artefacts (Sections 6–9). The catalogue was built and verified over **five extraction passes (the fifth a full cover-to-cover sweep of all 514 pages) plus an Appendix B (Techniques-to-Task) cross-check**, logged in Section 12.

## 5. Requirements & Designs classification artefacts (Chapter 2)

BABOK's **Requirements Classification Schema** (§2.3) defines the requirement *types* that BA work produces:

- **Business requirements** — goals, objectives, and outcomes describing *why* a change was initiated (enterprise, business area, or initiative level).
- **Stakeholder requirements** — needs of stakeholders that must be met to achieve the business requirements (bridge between business and solution).
- **Solution requirements** — capabilities/qualities a solution must have; divided into:
  - **Functional requirements** — behaviour and information the solution manages.
  - **Non-functional requirements** (quality of service / quality attributes) — conditions under which the solution must remain effective, or qualities it must have.
- **Transition requirements** — temporary capabilities/conditions needed to move from current to future state (e.g., data conversion, training, business continuity); no longer needed once the change is complete.

Related Chapter-2 concept: **Designs** — a usable representation of a solution, distinguished from requirements by intended use (a requirement focuses on the need; a design focuses on the solution). **Requirements and Designs** are the two artefact classes the guide manages throughout.

*Underpinning model (not a deliverable but the organising frame): the **Business Analysis Core Concept Model™ (BACCM™)** with six core concepts — Change, Need, Solution, Context, Stakeholder, Value.*

## 6. Packaging & representation artefacts

BABOK defines how business analysis information is **packaged** and **represented** — these are artefact/document forms in their own right (defined in the Glossary and in §7.1 *Specify and Model Requirements*).

**Packaging artefacts (Glossary):**
- **Business analysis package** — "a document, presentation, or other collection of text, matrices, diagrams and models, representing business analysis information." The general container used to communicate BA information to a stakeholder group.
- **Requirements package** — a specialized form of a business analysis package primarily concerned with requirements; may represent a **baseline** of a collection of requirements. (The Glossary lists **"requirements document" → *see requirements package***, so the two are synonymous.)

**Representation forms of requirements / BA information (§7.1 Elements):** every requirement or model is captured in one (or a mix) of these forms —
- **Text** — plain or structured textual requirements (e.g., business rules, requirement statements).
- **Matrices** — tabular representation; used for **data dictionaries**, **requirements traceability**, **gap analysis**, and **prioritization**.
- **Diagrams** — visual/pictorial representation conveying complexity words cannot.
- **Models** — grouped by the five **model categories**: *People and Roles · Rationale · Activity Flow · Capability · Data and Information.*

*Related state: a **requirements baseline** — a reviewed and agreed set of requirements fixed as a reference point.*

## 8. Procurement, solicitation & agreement documents

BABOK names several formal **solicitation and agreement documents** used mainly in elicitation, vendor assessment, and change strategy (defined in the Glossary and referenced in §10.4, §10.49, §6.4):

- **Request for Information (RFI)** — a formal method to collect information from potential suppliers.
- **Request for Proposal (RFP)** — "a requirements document issued when an organization is seeking a formal proposal from vendors"; itself a form of requirements document.
- **Request for Quote (RFQ)** — a procurement method soliciting price and solution information.
- **Request for Tender (RFT)** — a formal, structured invitation to bid.
- **Statement of Work (SOW)** — description of work/deliverables to be performed under contract.
- **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** — agreed measures of success / service constraints between provider and customer.
- **Scope statement** — clearly defines what is in and out of scope for the change.
- **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** — hierarchical decomposition of the work to be done (used in estimation and planning).

## 9. Analysis, assessment, review & test artefacts

Passes 4–5 (a full Glossary diff plus a task-*Elements* and cover-to-cover sweep) surfaced named **analysis, assessment, review, and test** artefacts that the task Input/Output model does not expose — several are defined only inside a task's *Elements* subsection or in the Glossary (Appendix A).

**Task-Element artefacts (named sub-deliverables inside a task):**
- **Enterprise Readiness Assessment** — element of *6.4 Define Change Strategy*; assesses whether the enterprise is prepared to make effective use of, and absorb, the solution.
- **Checklist(s)** — element of *7.2 Verify Requirements*; a quality-control aid used when verifying requirements and designs.
- **Impact analysis** — element of *5.4 Assess Requirements Changes*; assesses the effect of a proposed change on requirements, coverage, and allocation.
- **Gap analysis** — element of *6.4 Define Change Strategy*; a comparison of current state vs. desired future state.
- **Enterprise Culture Assessment** — element of *8.4 Assess Enterprise Limitations*; assesses beliefs, values, and norms that affect adoption.
- **Operational Assessment** — element of *8.4 Assess Enterprise Limitations*; determines whether the enterprise can adapt to and effectively use the solution.
- **Stakeholder Impact Analysis** — element of *8.4 Assess Enterprise Limitations*; assesses how the solution affects each stakeholder group.
- **Traceability Repository** — element of *5.1 Trace Requirements*; the store in which requirements traceability is documented and maintained.
- **Business Analysis Information Architecture** — element of *3.4 Plan Business Analysis Information Management*; the structure/organization of business analysis information.
- **Focus Group Plan** — element of *Focus Groups (10.21)*; ensures stakeholders agree on purpose, outcomes, and logistics of the session.
- **Definitional (structural) rules** and **Behavioural (operative) rules** — the two categories of business rules from *Business Rules Analysis (10.9)*.

**Analysis artefacts (Glossary / technique sub-forms):**
- **Cost-benefit analysis** — within *Financial Analysis (10.20)*.
- **Competitive analysis** — within *Benchmarking and Market Analysis (10.4)*.
- **Force field analysis** — driving vs. restraining forces for a change (Glossary-defined).
- **Feasibility study** — evaluation of the viability of options (Glossary-defined).
- **Risk assessment** — the assessment underlying *Risk Analysis Results (6.3)*.
- **Stakeholder analysis** — the analysis producing the *Stakeholder List, Map, or Personas (10.43)*.

**Test artefact:**
- **User acceptance test (UAT)** — acceptance testing performed by users to confirm the solution meets their requirements (Glossary-defined).

**Prototype sub-forms (Prototyping 10.36 / Glossary):**
- **Throw-away (rapid) prototype · Evolutionary (functional) prototype · Horizontal prototype · Vertical prototype · Proof of concept.**

**Review sub-forms (Reviews 10.37 / Glossary):**
- **Peer review · Inspection · Walkthrough** — the review types; each produces a review record / **defect** log.

**Requirements metadata & quality artefacts (Glossary):**
- **Requirements attribute(s)** — metadata about a requirement (e.g., priority, source, status, owner).
- **Requirements defect / defect** — a deficiency logged against a requirement or design during review.

**Meta-definitions (what the guide counts as an "artefact"):** BABOK's Glossary explicitly defines **artifact**, **work product**, **deliverable**, and **requirements artifact** — the umbrella vocabulary underlying every item in this catalogue.

**Plans defined in the Glossary (legacy / related — superseded in v3 by the "approaches" in Section 1):** **Business analysis plan**, **Business analysis communication plan**, **Requirements management plan**. Retained here because the Glossary still defines them and readers migrating from BABOK v2 will look for them.

## 11. Perspective-specific artefacts (Chapter 11)

### 11.1 Agile Perspective
- Product vision statement (a common initial deliverable)
- Product backlog; user stories; acceptance criteria
- Story map (from Story Mapping)
- Personas
- Value stream / value stream map (Value Stream Analysis)
- **Epics** and **features** (larger backlog items decomposed into stories)
- **Spikes** (time-boxed investigation items)
- **Release plan / release backlog**; **product roadmap**
- Lightweight ("barely sufficient") documentation
- Estimates (e.g., story points); MoSCoW prioritization output
- SAFe™ program/portfolio artefacts (when scaling agile)
- Purpose Alignment Model

### 11.2 Business Intelligence Perspective
- Source **logical data model** and source **data dictionary**
- Target **logical data model** and target **data dictionary**
- **Solution architecture** (high-level BI design view)
- Data sources inventory; **ETL** (Extract, Transform, Load) design
- **Data warehouse** / **data marts** design; physical data models
- **Dashboards**; **balanced scorecards**; KPI scorecards; reports/charts
- Metadata definitions; data-matching / data-quality specifications
- Reporting & information-delivery requirements (standard, ad-hoc, analytic)

### 11.3 Information Technology Perspective
- Current state (as-is) analysis of manual processes and systems
- Future state description and gap analysis
- Software/technical **design or blueprint** of the change
- Process models; use cases and scenarios; user stories
- Functional specifications; non-functional (quality-of-service) requirements
- Roles and permissions / data-security specifications

### 11.4 Business Architecture Perspective
Architectural **blueprints, models, and views**, including (Table 11.4.2):
- **Business capability map** (hierarchical catalogue of capabilities)
- **Value stream / value stream map** (Value Mapping)
- **Organizational map** (unit interactions, not the org chart)
- **Information map** (taxonomy of key business concepts)
- **Roadmap** (current → transition → future state)
- **Customer journey map**
- **Enterprise core diagram**
- Business Process Architecture
- Project Portfolio Analysis; Service-Oriented Analysis
- **Business Motivation Model (BMM)**
- Reference models & frameworks used as templates: **Zachman Framework**, **TOGAF®** (ADM Phase B), **Archimate®**, APQC Process Classification Framework, SCOR, Value Reference Model (VRM), FEA reference models, etc.
