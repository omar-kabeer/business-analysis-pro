# Conformance checks: iso-9241-210-2010

Source id: `iso-9241-210-2010`
Standard: ISO 9241-210, Ergonomics of human-system interaction, Part 210: Human-centred design for interactive systems.
Edition held: First edition, 2010-03-15 (ISO 9241-210:2010(E)).
Publisher: ISO.
Identity: confirmed from the title page on 2026-08-07. First edition 2010. A 2019 revision supersedes this edition; the checks below are for the 2010 edition held, and should be cited as such.
Authority: normative.
Licence: ISO copyright, restricted (the copy carries an IHS licence stamp). Paraphrase only.
Modality note: unlike ISO 31000, this standard states its human-centred design process requirements with "shall". Those map to blocking checks. Its "should" items are advisory.
Artefact types constrained: `design-process`, `prototype`.
Owning skills: `ux`, `prototyping`.
Locator style: ISO 9241-210:2010 clause `<n>`.

## How to read this file

Three layers, kept separate: Source (S) with locator, labelled Inference (I), and the Check (C), a yes-or-no question answerable from the finished artefact.

## Checks: design-process

Applies to a human-centred design process for an interactive system.

| Check id | Question (yes = pass) | Severity | Locator | How the reviewer confirms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO9241-DP-01 | Does the process carry out all four human-centred design activities: understanding the context of use, specifying user requirements, producing design solutions, and evaluating the design? | blocking | 6.1, 6.2 to 6.5 | Look for evidence of all four activities. A process missing any of the four fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-02 | Does a context-of-use description exist covering the users and stakeholder groups, the users' characteristics, their goals and tasks, and the environments (technical, physical, social, cultural)? | blocking | 6.2.2 | Check the context description contains all four elements. A missing element fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-03 | Is there an explicit statement of user requirements covering the intended context of use, needs-derived requirements, requirements from ergonomics standards and guidelines (including accessibility), usability requirements with measurable performance and satisfaction criteria, and organizational requirements affecting the user? | blocking | 6.3.3 | Check the user requirements statement contains all five categories. Missing measurable usability criteria, or any category, fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-04 | Is user-centred evaluation carried out, including early evaluation of design concepts, to drive the design? | blocking | 6.5.1 | Confirm evaluation occurred and fed the design. A process with no user-centred evaluation fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-05 | Is the process iterative, with descriptions, specifications, and prototypes revised as new information is obtained? | blocking | 4.5, 6.1 | Look for more than one design iteration incorporating feedback. A single-pass design fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-06 | Is human-centred design planned and integrated into the project plan, with time and resources allocated for the HCD activities and for iteration and user feedback? | blocking | 5.1 to 5.5 | Confirm an HCD plan within the project plan, with allocated time including iteration. Absence fails. |
| ISO9241-DP-07 | Does the design team include multidisciplinary skills and perspectives? | advisory | 4.7 | Team composition may not be inspectable from the artefact; record what the artefact evidences. |
| ISO9241-DP-08 | Does the design address the whole user experience, not only the user interface? | advisory | 4.6 | Look for attention to the wider experience. Advisory judgement. |
| ISO9241-DP-09 | Is the user requirements specification stated in testable terms, verified by stakeholders, internally consistent, and kept updated? | advisory | 6.3.5 | The standard states these with "should". Flag gaps as quality issues. |

## Checks: prototype

Applies to a prototype produced within a human-centred design process.

| Check id | Question (yes = pass) | Severity | Locator | How the reviewer confirms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO9241-PR-01 | Is the prototype used to make a design solution concrete and to obtain user feedback through evaluation, rather than only to demonstrate the design? | blocking | 6.4.1, 6.5 | Confirm the prototype fed a user-centred evaluation. A prototype built but never evaluated with users fails. |
| ISO9241-PR-02 | Is the prototype's level of detail and realism appropriate to the design issues being investigated? | advisory | 6.4.3 | Judge fidelity against the questions the prototype must answer. Advisory. |
| ISO9241-PR-03 | When the prototype is tested, do users carry out tasks using it rather than being shown a demonstration? | advisory | 6.5.4 | Check the evaluation had users performing tasks. Advisory method check. |

### Provenance, design-process

- DP-01. S: Four linked human-centred design activities shall take place during the design of any interactive system: understanding and specifying the context of use, specifying the user requirements, producing design solutions, and evaluating the design (6.1, 6.2 to 6.5). I: We gate on evidence of all four.
- DP-02. S: The context-of-use description shall include the users and other stakeholder groups, the characteristics of the users, the goals and tasks of the users, and the environments of the system (6.2.2 a to d, each stated with "shall"). I: none.
- DP-03. S: This activity shall be extended to create an explicit statement of user requirements; the specification of user requirements shall include the intended context of use; requirements derived from user needs and context; requirements from ergonomics and user interface knowledge, standards and guidelines including accessibility; usability requirements and objectives with measurable performance and satisfaction criteria; and organizational requirements that directly affect the user (6.3.2, 6.3.3 a to e). I: none.
- DP-04. S: User-centred evaluation is a required activity in human-centred design; even at the earliest stages, design concepts should be evaluated (6.5.1). I: We gate on evaluation occurring, since it is one of the four "shall" activities.
- DP-05. S: The process is iterative; iteration implies that descriptions, specifications, and prototypes are revised and refined when new information is obtained (4.5). I: We gate on evidence of iteration.
- DP-06. S: Human-centred design shall be planned and integrated into all phases; the plan shall form part of the overall project plan; project planning shall allocate time and resources to human-centred activities, including time for iteration and incorporation of user feedback (5.1 to 5.5). I: none.
- DP-07. S: The design team includes multidisciplinary skills and perspectives (4.7). I: Team composition is often not inspectable from the artefact, so advisory.
- DP-08. S: The design addresses the whole user experience (4.6). I: Advisory judgement.
- DP-09. S: The user requirements specification should be stated in terms that permit testing, verified by stakeholders, internally consistent, and updated during the project (6.3.5). I: Stated with "should", so advisory.

### Provenance, prototype

- PR-01. S: Producing design solutions includes making them concrete using prototypes and mock-ups, and altering them in response to user-centred evaluation; when prototypes are tested, the information gathered is used to drive the design (6.4.1, 6.5). I: We gate on the prototype serving evaluation, since that is its role in HCD.
- PR-02. S: The level of detail and realism should be appropriate to the issues that need to be investigated; over-investing in a detailed prototype can cause reluctance to change (6.4.3). I: Advisory.
- PR-03. S: When prototypes are being tested, users should carry out tasks using the prototype rather than be shown demonstrations (6.5.4). I: Advisory method check.

## Coverage

- `design-process`: 6 blocking checks (DP-01 to DP-06), 3 advisory (DP-07 to DP-09).
- `prototype`: 1 blocking check (PR-01), 2 advisory (PR-02, PR-03).
