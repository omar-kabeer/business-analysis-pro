# Conformance checks: iso-29148

Source id: `iso-29148`
Standard: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, Systems and software engineering, Life cycle processes, Requirements engineering.
Edition held: First edition, 2011-12-01 (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2011(E)).
Publisher: ISO/IEC/IEEE.
Authority: normative.
Identity: confirmed from the title page on 2026-08-07. The copy is the 2011 first edition, not the 2018 second edition. Cite the edition held.
Artefact types constrained: `requirement-statement`, `requirements-package`.
Owning skill: `requirements`.
Locator style: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 clause `<n>`.

## How to read this file

Every check is built from three layers that are kept separate so a reviewer can audit the reasoning.

- Source (S). What the standard actually says, paraphrased, with the clause locator. Never our opinion.
- Inference (I). Our reasoning that connects the clause to our artefacts. Labelled as inference because the standard does not state it in these words.
- Check (C). The instruction to the reviewer, stated as a question that a finished artefact answers yes or no without the reviewer opening the standard.

The check tables below carry the Check and its locator. The provenance section that follows each table records the Source paraphrase and any Inference the check rests on. Where a check adds nothing beyond the clause, the inference is marked none.

Severity: blocking means the artefact fails conformance if the answer is no. Advisory means the answer is recorded and weighed by a reviewer but does not by itself fail the artefact, either because the standard states it as guidance rather than a requirement, or because the answer needs context the artefact alone does not carry.

## Checks: requirement-statement

Applies to a single requirement statement.

| Check id | Question (yes = pass) | Severity | Locator | How the reviewer confirms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO29148-RS-01 | Does the statement have an explicit subject and an action, in the form subject + verb + object or an equivalent structured form (condition-action table, use case)? | blocking | 5.2.4 | Read the statement. Point to the subject (the system or element) and the action it must perform. A fragment with no actor or no action fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-02 | Is the statement singular, that is one requirement with no conjunction joining two obligations? | blocking | 5.2.5 | Look for "and", "or", commas that join separate obligations. If the statement could be split into two testable requirements, it fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-03 | Is the statement free of vague or unbounded terms (superlatives, subjective language, vague pronouns, ambiguous adverbs and adjectives, open-ended phrases, comparatives, loopholes, negative capability statements)? | blocking | 5.2.7 | Scan against the banned-term list in the provenance below. Any hit that is not quantified or defined fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-04 | Can the statement be interpreted in only one way? | blocking | 5.2.5 | After removing vague terms, ask whether two reviewers would read it identically. If a second reading exists, it fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-05 | Is the statement verifiable, that is does it carry a measurable condition or value against which the system can be shown to satisfy it? | blocking | 5.2.5, 5.2.4 | Identify the measurable condition or the verification means. A statement that cannot be proven by test, inspection, analysis, or demonstration fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-06 | Is the statement implementation-free, stating what is needed rather than a design or technology solution? | blocking | 5.2.5, 5.2.7 | Check that the statement does not name a specific product, component, or design method as the requirement itself. Named constraints that are genuine stakeholder constraints are allowed if recorded as such. |
| ISO29148-RS-07 | Is the statement complete, needing no further amplification and containing no TBD, TBS, or TBR placeholder? | blocking | 5.2.5, 5.2.6 | Search the statement for TBD, TBS, TBR, TBx, "to be defined", and blanks. Any placeholder fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-08 | Is the statement traceable upward to a stated need or higher-tier requirement, and downward to lower-tier requirements or verification, by explicit link or identifier? | blocking | 5.2.5 | Confirm a parent reference (need, higher requirement, or source) and a forward link exist. A statement with no trace either way fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-09 | If the statement rests on an assumption, is that assumption recorded in a requirement attribute (for example rationale) or an accompanying document? | blocking | 5.2.7 | Where the statement only holds under an assumption, look for that assumption in the rationale or attributes. An unrecorded assumption fails. |
| ISO29148-RS-10 | Does the statement express a need that is currently applicable, with any planned expiry or applicability date stated? | advisory | 5.2.5 | Necessity in full needs the trace and stakeholder context. Confirm the statement is not obsolete and that any time-limited applicability is noted. |
| ISO29148-RS-11 | Is the statement technically feasible within cost, schedule, technical, legal, and regulatory constraints at acceptable risk? | advisory | 5.2.5 | Feasibility needs solution context the statement alone does not carry. Record the reviewer judgement rather than failing the artefact on wording. |
| ISO29148-RS-12 | Does the statement use the mandatory keyword "shall" for a binding requirement, reserving will, should, and may for non-binding provisions, and avoid "must"? | advisory | 5.2.4 | The standard presents the keyword scheme as common practice. Flag a binding requirement that does not use "shall", but treat it as guidance. |
| ISO29148-RS-13 | Is the statement positive and in the active voice, avoiding "shall not" and passive constructions? | advisory | 5.2.4, 5.2.7 | Presented as guidance. Flag negative or passive phrasing for rework without failing on it alone. |
| ISO29148-RS-14 | Is the statement uniquely identified by a stable identifier that is never reused or changed? | advisory | 5.2.8.1 | The standard states identification with "should". Confirm an id exists and is unique. See RS-08: traceability in practice depends on it. |
| ISO29148-RS-15 | Does any reference in the statement cite the referenced document with its date and version and the applicable part? | advisory | 5.2.7 | Where the statement points to another document, confirm date, version, and the specific part. Bare references are an ambiguity flag. |

### Provenance, requirement-statement

- RS-01. S: A well-formed requirement, if in natural language, comprises a subject, a verb, and a complement; a requirement shall state the subject and what shall be done; condition-action tables and use cases are other means (5.2.4). I: We read "shall state the subject" as a mandatory structural test a reviewer can apply to the wording. C as stated.
- RS-02. S: Singular. The requirement statement includes only one requirement with no use of conjunctions (5.2.5). I: none.
- RS-03. S: Vague and general terms shall be avoided; the standard lists unbounded or ambiguous term types: superlatives (best, most); subjective language (user friendly, easy to use, cost effective); vague pronouns (it, this, that); ambiguous adverbs and adjectives (almost always, significant, minimal); open-ended non-verifiable terms (provide support, but not limited to, as a minimum); comparative phrases (better than, higher quality); loopholes (if possible, as appropriate, as applicable); negative statements of capability (5.2.7). I: none. The banned-term list is the reviewer's checklist.
- RS-04. S: Unambiguous. The requirement is stated so it can be interpreted in only one way (5.2.5). I: We treat single-interpretation as testable once RS-03 has removed vague terms.
- RS-05. S: Verifiable. The requirement has the means to prove the system satisfies it; verifiability is enhanced when measurable. A well-formed requirement can be verified and is qualified by measurable conditions and bounded by constraints (5.2.5, 5.2.4). I: none.
- RS-06. S: Implementation Free. The requirement states what is required, not how; it avoids placing unnecessary constraints on the architectural design (5.2.5). Requirements should state what, not how (5.2.7). I: Genuine stakeholder constraints that name a technology are permitted where recorded as constraints (5.2.4 on constraints); we surface that exception so the check does not reject legitimate constraints.
- RS-07. S: Complete. The requirement needs no further amplification because it is measurable and sufficiently describes the capability (5.2.5). The set contains no TBD, TBS, or TBR (5.2.6). I: We apply the set-level ban on placeholders to the individual statement as a mechanical search.
- RS-08. S: Traceable. The requirement is upward traceable to a documented stakeholder need, higher-tier requirement, or other source, and downward traceable to lower-tier requirements or other definition artefacts; all parent-child relationships are identified (5.2.5). I: none.
- RS-09. S: All assumptions made regarding a requirement shall be documented and validated in one of the requirement's attributes (for example rationale) or in an accompanying document (5.2.7). I: none.
- RS-10. S: Necessary. The requirement defines an essential capability; if removed a deficiency exists; it is currently applicable and not made obsolete; requirements with planned expiration or applicability dates are clearly identified (5.2.5). I: Full necessity (that removal causes a deficiency) needs the trace and stakeholder context, so we score it advisory and test the observable parts: current applicability and stated expiry.
- RS-11. S: Feasible. The requirement is technically achievable and fits within constraints with acceptable risk (5.2.5). I: Feasibility depends on solution and technology context outside the statement, so it is advisory.
- RS-12. S: A common approach stipulates shall for mandatory binding provisions, will for statements of fact, should for preferences, may for allowances, and avoids must; use "shall" for requirements (5.2.4). I: The standard frames this as a common approach and best practice, not a shall, so advisory.
- RS-13. S: Use positive statements and avoid negative requirements such as shall not; use active voice and avoid passive voice (5.2.4). Negative statements are listed among ambiguous terms (5.2.7). I: Presented as guidance, so advisory.
- RS-14. S: Each requirement should be uniquely identified; once assigned the identifier is never changed or reused (5.2.8.1). It is important to include unique identifiers for each requirement (6.3.3.2). I: The standard uses "should", so advisory, but RS-08 traceability is not auditable in practice without it.
- RS-15. S: Incomplete references, not specifying the reference with its date and version number, or not specifying the applicable parts, are among the ambiguous terms to avoid (5.2.7). I: none.

## Checks: requirements-package

Applies to a requirements specification as a whole (StRS, SyRS, or SRS) and to any delivered set of requirements.

| Check id | Question (yes = pass) | Severity | Locator | How the reviewer confirms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO29148-RP-01 | Is the set complete, containing everything pertinent to the system being specified and no TBD, TBS, or TBR clause? | blocking | 5.2.6 | Search the whole package for TBD, TBS, TBR, TBx, and open placeholders. Any unresolved placeholder without a recorded resolution timeframe fails. |
| ISO29148-RP-02 | Is the set internally consistent, with no contradictory requirements, no duplicated requirements, and the same term used for the same item throughout? | blocking | 5.2.6 | Scan for conflicting obligations, repeated requirements, and terms used two ways. A contradiction or a term collision fails. |
| ISO29148-RP-03 | Does the package contain the content defined for its document type in clause 9 (StRS 9.3, SyRS 9.4, SRS 9.5), including identification, front matter, definitions, references, and acronyms? | blocking | 7, 9.2 | Compare the package structure against the clause 9 outline for its type, listed in the provenance below. A missing mandatory content area fails. |
| ISO29148-RP-04 | Are the requirements under configuration control, with a version or baseline identifier and a recorded change history? | blocking | 6.5.2.1 | Confirm a version or baseline id and a change record. An uncontrolled or undated set fails. |
| ISO29148-RP-05 | In an StRS, are business operational policies and rules each uniquely named and numbered and referenced from the process descriptions? | blocking | 9.3.10 | For an StRS, check each policy or rule has a unique name and number and is cited where the process uses it. Applies only to StRS packages. |
| ISO29148-RP-06 | Is the set affordable, satisfiable by a solution obtainable within life cycle constraints (cost, schedule, technical, legal, regulatory)? | advisory | 5.2.6 | Affordability needs cost and schedule context the package alone does not carry. Record the reviewer judgement. |
| ISO29148-RP-07 | Is the set bounded, holding to the intended scope without expanding beyond what stakeholder needs require? | advisory | 5.2.6 | Scope growth is a judgement against stakeholder need. Flag apparent scope creep for review rather than failing on wording. |
| ISO29148-RP-08 | Is there bidirectional traceability across the set, for example a requirements traceability matrix linking each requirement to its source and forward to verification? | advisory | 6.3.3.2 | The standard states traceability with "should" at set level. Confirm an RTM or equivalent links requirements both ways. |

### Provenance, requirements-package

- RP-01. S: Complete. The set contains everything pertinent to the definition of the system and contains no TBD, TBS, or TBR clauses; resolution may be iterative with an acceptable timeframe (5.2.6). I: We allow a placeholder that carries a recorded resolution timeframe, which the clause explicitly permits.
- RP-02. S: Consistent. The set has no contradictory requirements, requirements are not duplicated, and the same term is used for the same item in all requirements (5.2.6). I: none.
- RP-03. S: The project shall produce the StRS, SyRS, and (under ISO/IEC 12207) SRS, and the information items shall contain the content as defined in clause 9 (clause 7). Clause 9.2 sets general content: identification, front matter, definitions, references, acronyms and abbreviations. Clause 9.3 lists StRS content, 9.4 SyRS content, 9.5 SRS content (7, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4, 9.5). I: We treat the clause 9 outline for the package's declared type as the content checklist. Reviewer references the outline for that type; the mandatory areas are the numbered subclauses under 9.3, 9.4, or 9.5.
- RP-04. S: The requirements shall be configuration controlled; requirements shall be configuration managed in accordance with project and organization configuration management processes; ancillary information may include a change history (6.5.2.1). I: Presence of a baseline or version id and a change record is our observable proxy for configuration control on a delivered package.
- RP-05. S: Business operational policies and rules shall be uniquely named and numbered, and shall be referenced in the description of the business processes (9.3.10). I: Scoped to StRS because 9.3 is StRS content.
- RP-06. S: Affordable. The complete set can be satisfied by a solution obtainable within life cycle constraints (5.2.6). I: Needs cost and schedule context, so advisory.
- RP-07. S: Bounded. The set maintains the identified scope without increasing beyond what is needed to satisfy user needs (5.2.6). I: Scope judgement against stakeholder need, so advisory.
- RP-08. S: Demonstrate traceability between system and stakeholder requirements; maintain bidirectional traceability; traceability may be documented in a requirements traceability matrix (6.3.3.2). I: The clause uses "should" and "may" for the RTM at set level, so advisory. Item-level traceability is blocking under RS-08.
