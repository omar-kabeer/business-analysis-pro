# Discovery Frameworks

Frameworks for understanding the problem before committing to a solution. Used by the product-manager and elicitation skills and the `opportunity-solution-tree` template.

## Jobs to be Done

- Purpose: understand the progress a customer is trying to make.
- When to use: framing needs and value, and sharpening product-market fit.
- Method: capture the job in context: when [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]. Study the circumstances, not just demographics.
- Pitfalls: writing features as jobs; ignoring the situation that triggers the job.

## Opportunity Solution Tree

- Purpose: connect a desired outcome to opportunities, solutions, and experiments.
- When to use: continuous discovery where you want solutions tied to real needs.
- Method: outcome at the root; opportunities (unmet needs, pains, desires) as branches from research; solutions under opportunities; experiments under solutions. Do not attach a solution with no opportunity above it.
- Pitfalls: jumping to one solution; opportunities sourced from opinion, not evidence.

## Continuous Discovery

- Purpose: keep learning small and frequent rather than in big upfront phases.
- When to use: any product team that can talk to customers regularly.
- Method: weekly customer touchpoints, assumption mapping, and quick tests (interview, prototype, fake door, concierge). De-risk the biggest unknowns first across value, usability, feasibility, and viability.
- Pitfalls: research theatre with no decisions; testing safe assumptions instead of risky ones.
