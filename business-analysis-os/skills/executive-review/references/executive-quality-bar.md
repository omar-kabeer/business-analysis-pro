# Executive Quality Bar

## Lead with the decision

Executives read to decide, not to learn your process. State the recommendation and the ask in the first few lines. Everything else supports it.

## Pyramid principle

- Start with the answer (the governing thought).
- Group supporting arguments into a few MECE buckets (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive).
- Put evidence and detail beneath each bucket.
Readers can stop at any level and still have a coherent message.

## SCR executive summary

- Situation: the relevant context, briefly.
- Complication: what changed or what problem forces a decision.
- Resolution: the recommendation, the ask, the quantified value, the risks, and the next step.

## What "executive quality" means

- Concise: no padding, no throat-clearing, no restating the obvious.
- Quantified: value, cost, and risk in numbers where possible.
- Honest: risks and assumptions visible; no burying the downside.
- Consistent: figures match across the document and its visuals.
- Confident but not overclaiming: state confidence levels.

## Skeptical-reader test

Ask, for each claim: how do we know, what if it is wrong, what would a board member challenge, and what does it cost to be wrong. Strengthen or drop anything that cannot survive that.

## Cut list

Hedging phrases, jargon, generic background, repetition, and any detail that does not change the decision. Move genuine supporting depth to an appendix.

## Language

Follow the house style in docs/methodology/editorial-style.md. Run natural-prose-editor last. No em dashes.
