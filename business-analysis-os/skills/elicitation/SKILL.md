# Elicitation and Discovery Skill

## Role

Prepare for, structure, and get the most from elicitation: interviews, workshops, observation, document analysis, and surveys. Draw out real needs, expose assumptions and unknowns, and produce inputs that downstream analysis and requirements can rely on.

## Inputs

- The problem area, goals, and what is already known or assumed.
- Stakeholders and their roles, availability, and influence.
- Constraints on time, access, and format.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the discovery objective: what decision or requirement this elicitation must inform.
2. Identify stakeholders and choose techniques to match (see `references/elicitation-techniques.md`).
3. Build the elicitation plan: who, technique, sequence, logistics, and the questions each session must answer.
4. Draft the instruments: interview scripts, workshop agendas with timeboxes and activities, or questionnaires. Use open, non-leading questions and probe for the "why".
5. Capture outputs live: needs, pain points, decisions, assumptions, unknowns, and conflicts. Separate fact from opinion.
6. Confirm understanding with stakeholders and hand a clean, sourced input pack to analysis or requirements.

## Techniques

Interviews (structured and unstructured), facilitated workshops, observation and job shadowing, document and data analysis, surveys, prototyping, and brainstorming. Selection guidance and question banks are in `references/elicitation-techniques.md`.

## Outputs

- An elicitation or discovery plan.
- Interview scripts, workshop agendas, or questionnaires.
- A structured capture of needs, assumptions, unknowns, risks, and open questions.

## Quality gates

- Questions are open and non-leading and map to the discovery objective.
- Assumptions and unknowns are recorded explicitly, not buried.
- Fact is separated from opinion and each point is attributed to a source.
- Coverage across the relevant stakeholder groups, not just the loudest voice.

## Done when

The discovery objective is covered, instruments are ready or sessions are captured, and a clean, sourced input pack with explicit assumptions and unknowns is ready for analysis.

## House style

Run the `natural-prose-editor` pass on written instruments and summaries and use no em dashes. See `docs/methodology/editorial-style.md`.
