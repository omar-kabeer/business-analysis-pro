# Product Frameworks

Structured lenses for discovery, strategy, prioritisation, and lifecycle governance.

## DHM: Delight, Hard-to-copy, Margin (Gibson Biddle)

Test every strategic bet against three long-term questions so the roadmap builds enterprise value, not just short-term features:

- Delight: does it exceed expectations and solve real pain in a memorable way (for example personalised recommendations, one-click checkout)?
- Hard-to-copy advantage: does it build a durable moat competitors cannot easily replicate? The seven moats are network effects, economies of scale, brand equity, unique technology, counter-positioning, switching costs, and process power.
- Margin: does it sustain or grow profitability and fund future innovation (for example subscription shift, tier limits, high-margin enterprise add-ons)?

A bet that only delights, with no defensibility or margin, is the feature-factory trap.

## Prioritisation

- RICE: Reach times Impact times Confidence, divided by Effort. State the scales.
- Value vs Effort matrix: a 2x2 giving quick wins (high value, low effort), major projects (high value, high effort), fill-ins (low value, low effort), and thankless tasks (low value, high effort).
- Kano: basic, performance, and delight needs. Cover basics before investing in delight.
- Opportunity score: rank customer problems by importance and satisfaction; prioritise important, underserved problems.

Tie whichever method you use back to the north-star outcome. See also `frameworks/prioritisation.md` at the repository root.

## Win/Loss analysis

Interview recent buyers who chose you or a competitor to learn why. It reveals sales-cycle mechanics, pricing sensitivity, messaging resonance, and competitive feature gaps. Feed findings into positioning, roadmap, and pricing.

## Pragmatic Framework (37-box lifecycle)

An organisational standard for market-driven products across seven categories:

- Market: find urgent market problems, run win/loss, assess competitors and distinctive competencies.
- Focus: define target segments, channels, portfolio strategy, and the roadmap.
- Business: build the business case, pricing and packaging, buy-build-partner decisions, and profitability tracking.
- Planning: define buyer and user personas, market requirements, use scenarios, and stakeholder communication.
- Programs: go-to-market plans, launch programs, campaign measurement, and advocacy.
- Enablement: sales alignment, sales tools, enablement content, and channel training.
- Support: customer operations, logistics, events, and post-launch feedback loops.

Use it to check that a product effort covers the full market-to-support lifecycle, not just build.

## Product lifecycle stages

Concept, launch, growth, maturity, and sunset. The PM balances long-term vision with short-term execution and shifts emphasis by stage: discovery and fit early, growth and monetisation in the middle, efficiency and eventual retirement later.
