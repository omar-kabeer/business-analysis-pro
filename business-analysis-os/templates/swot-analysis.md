# SWOT Analysis

## Purpose

Assess an enterprise, unit, or initiative across its internal strengths and weaknesses and its external opportunities and threats, then turn that assessment into strategy. This is the BABOK SWOT Analysis technique (10.46). A SWOT is only useful if it is evidence-based and each item leads to an action; a generic SWOT is worthless.

## Inputs

The current state assessment, market and competitor context, performance data, and stakeholder input.

## 1. SWOT grid

Strengths and weaknesses are internal factors the enterprise controls. Opportunities and threats are external factors in the environment. Keep each item specific and supported by evidence.

| Internal | External |
| --- | --- |
| Strengths: what the enterprise does well and can build on | Opportunities: external conditions it could exploit |
| Weaknesses: internal limitations to fix or manage | Threats: external conditions that could harm it |

## 2. Strategy generation (TOWS)

Match internal and external factors to generate candidate strategies. This is what turns a static grid into decisions.

| | Opportunities | Threats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strengths | SO: use strengths to seize opportunities | ST: use strengths to counter threats |
| Weaknesses | WO: fix weaknesses to seize opportunities | WT: minimise weaknesses and avoid threats |

## Review criteria

- Each item is specific and supported by evidence, not a generic label.
- Internal and external factors are correctly separated.
- The analysis produces candidate strategies through the TOWS matching, not just four lists.
- Strategies are prioritised and hand off to finance or the roadmap where relevant.
