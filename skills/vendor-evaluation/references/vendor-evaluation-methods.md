# Vendor Evaluation Methods

## Criteria design

Group criteria so the picture is complete:

- Functional fit: how well the product meets the requirements.
- Non-functional: security, scalability, availability, performance, accessibility, support and SLAs.
- Commercial: price, licensing model, contract terms, total cost of ownership.
- Vendor viability: financial stability, track record, references, roadmap.
- Risk and compliance: data protection, jurisdiction, lock-in, exit terms.

Separate must-haves (a miss eliminates the vendor) from weighted criteria (scored and summed).

## RFI and RFP

- RFI (request for information): gather capability and background to build a longlist and refine requirements. Lightweight.
- RFP (request for proposal): ask a shortlist for detailed, structured proposals against your requirements so responses are comparable. Include the requirements, the evaluation criteria and weights, the response format, and the timeline.

## Weighted scoring matrix

1. List the criteria and assign a weight to each (weights sum to 100).
2. Agree a scoring scale (for example 0 to 5) and what each score means.
3. Score each vendor per criterion, multiply by the weight, and sum.
4. Record the evidence for each score.

Set the weights and scale before seeing responses so the process cannot be bent to a preferred vendor. Two evaluators scoring independently and reconciling reduces bias.

## Demos and proof of concept

Where capability claims are central or hard to judge on paper, run a scripted demo or a time-boxed proof of concept against a fixed scenario, and score it on the same criteria. Use the same scenario for every vendor.

## Total cost of ownership

Compare cost over the ownership period, not headline price: licence or subscription, implementation, integration, training, support, and exit or migration costs. Hand the modelling to the finance skill.

## Evaluation report

Include: the need and criteria, the shortlist, the scoring matrix, cost comparison, risks and due-diligence findings, and a clear recommendation naming the runner-up and why it lost. Decision makers should be able to follow the reasoning without redoing the work.
