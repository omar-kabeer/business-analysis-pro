# Intake Questions

Ask only what you cannot infer from what the user already said. Keep it to a short guided dialogue, a few questions at a time, in plain language. The goal is to classify the project and find the real need, not to run a full elicitation. For deep stakeholder elicitation, hand off to the `elicitation` skill.

## The eight intake dimensions

1. **Outcome.** What business outcome or problem is this project about? What changes for the business if it succeeds? Push past the feature to the result.
2. **Trigger.** Why now? Is it cost, growth, a competitor, a regulation, a failing system, a customer complaint, or a leadership mandate? The trigger often reveals the real need.
3. **Stage.** Where are you today: a raw idea, a case seeking approval, mid-analysis, designing, building, testing, or already live and measuring value?
4. **Solution type.** Is this a new build, buying or selecting a vendor package, a process change, a data or reporting initiative, a system replacement or migration, an integration, or an organisational change? A blend is fine.
5. **Delivery approach.** Agile, waterfall, or hybrid? This sets how heavy and how sequenced the documents should be.
6. **Domain and regulation.** What industry, and does any compliance regime apply (for example healthcare, payments, privacy, financial reporting)? This pulls in a domain pack and compliance artefacts.
7. **Decision audience.** Who signs off and who consumes the deliverables: a board, a steering committee, a PMO, a delivery team, auditors, or end users? This sets tone and format.
8. **Constraints.** Time, budget, team size and skills, access to stakeholders and data, and any fixed deadline or hard boundary.

## Reading the answers

- Stated request minus underlying need is the most common trap. Play the need back in one or two lines and confirm it before prescribing anything.
- Use trigger plus outcome to sense the archetype. Use stage to decide which documents are due now versus later. Use delivery approach and audience to right-size depth.
- If the answers are thin and the user cannot yet articulate the problem, that itself is the finding: route to the `elicitation` skill to run structured discovery before prescribing a document set.
