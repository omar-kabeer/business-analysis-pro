# Strategy Frameworks

Pick the framework that answers the question at hand. Using one well beats using five for show.

## Environment and market

- PESTLE: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental forces. Use to scan the macro environment before a market entry or major bet.
- Porter's Five Forces: rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. Use to judge the structural attractiveness of a market and where profit pools sit.

## Position

- SWOT: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. Keep it evidence-based and tie each item to an action; a generic SWOT is worthless.

## Business model

- Business Model Canvas: customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue streams, key resources, activities, partners, cost structure. Use to describe or redesign how value is created and captured.
- Lean Canvas: problem, solution, key metrics, unique value proposition, unfair advantage, channels, segments, cost, revenue. Better for early-stage or new ventures.
- Value Proposition Canvas: match customer jobs, pains, and gains to your pain relievers and gain creators. Use to sharpen product-market fit.

## Objectives and measurement

- OKRs: a qualitative Objective plus 3 to 5 measurable Key Results. Use to make strategy executable and trackable.
- Balanced Scorecard: financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth perspectives. Use for a rounded performance view.

## Options and decisions

- Options or scenario analysis: define genuinely distinct options, score them against strategic fit, value, feasibility, risk, and cost, and state the conditions under which the ranking would flip.

## Misuse to avoid

Framework theatre (running a canvas that changes nothing), confirmation bias (one option dressed as several), and analysis with no decision attached. Every framework output should move a decision.
