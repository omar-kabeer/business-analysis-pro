# Elicitation Techniques

## Choosing a technique

- Deep individual insight, sensitive topics: one-to-one interview.
- Shared understanding, cross-functional decisions, speed: facilitated workshop.
- How work really happens vs how people describe it: observation or job shadowing.
- Existing rules, data, and prior decisions: document and data analysis.
- Breadth across many stakeholders: survey or questionnaire.
- Validate a concept quickly: prototype or wireframe walkthrough.

## Interview question patterns

- Context: walk me through how you do this today. What happens before and after?
- Pain: where does this break down? What is the workaround?
- Goal: what does a good outcome look like? How would you measure it?
- Why: keep asking why to reach the root need, not the requested feature.
- Edge: when does the normal process not apply? What is the exception?
- Priority: if you could fix only one thing, what would it be?

Keep questions open (avoid yes or no), non-leading (do not embed the answer), and one at a time.

## Workshop agenda skeleton

1. Purpose and desired outcome (stated up front).
2. Context and current-state walkthrough.
3. Structured activity (process mapping, affinity grouping, prioritisation).
4. Divergent capture, then convergent decision.
5. Assumptions, risks, and open questions parked visibly.
6. Recap, decisions, owners, and next steps.

Timebox each item. Assign a facilitator and a scribe.

## Capturing output

Record needs, assumptions, unknowns, decisions, conflicts, and risks in separate buckets. Attribute each to a source. Confirm the summary with participants before it flows into analysis.

## Common failure modes

Leading questions, solutioning too early, capturing only the loudest stakeholder, mistaking opinion for fact, and leaving assumptions implicit.
