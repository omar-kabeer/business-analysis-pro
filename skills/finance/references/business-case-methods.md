# Business Case and Investment Methods

## Cash flow model

List one-off costs, recurring costs, and quantified benefits (new revenue, cost savings, avoided cost, risk reduction) by period. Be explicit about timing; money later is worth less than money now.

## Core metrics

- NPV (Net Present Value): sum of discounted net cash flows minus initial investment. Discount each period by 1 / (1 + r)^t. NPV greater than zero means the investment beats the discount rate. Prefer NPV as the primary decision metric.
- IRR (Internal Rate of Return): the discount rate at which NPV equals zero. Compare to the hurdle rate. Watch for multiple IRRs with unconventional cash flows.
- Payback period: time to recover the initial outlay. Simple and intuitive but ignores time value and everything after payback; use as a secondary lens.
- ROI: (net benefit / cost) as a percentage. Easy to communicate; specify the period it covers.

## Discount rate

Use the organisation's cost of capital or stated hurdle rate. If unknown, assume a reasonable rate and flag it clearly; the result is sensitive to this choice.

## Sensitivity and scenario analysis

- Sensitivity: vary one driver at a time (price, volume, cost, adoption, discount rate) and show the effect on NPV. Identify what the case hinges on.
- Scenario: coherent base, downside, and upside cases with bundled assumptions.
- Break-even: the driver value at which NPV equals zero.

## Risk adjustment and intangibles

Adjust cash flows or the discount rate for risk where justified, and document the logic. Record intangible benefits and costs (brand, morale, strategic optionality, compliance exposure) qualitatively so they are not lost.

## Honesty checks

Do not double-count benefits, do not ignore ongoing costs, do not present a single point estimate as certainty, and do not tune assumptions backward from a desired answer.
