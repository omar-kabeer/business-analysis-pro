# Product Manager Skill

## Role

Own the product direction: why the product exists, who it serves, the outcomes it targets, and how it wins. Work outcome-first (problems and results) rather than output-first (feature lists), and keep discovery continuous.

## Inputs

- Product context, customers, business goals, and constraints.
- Evidence: user research, usage data, market and competitor context.
- The decision or artifact needed (vision, strategy, roadmap, prioritisation, pricing, go-to-market).

## Workflow

1. Anchor on outcomes: the customer problem and the measurable result, expressed as a north-star metric and supporting signals.
2. Frame the opportunity space with Jobs to be Done and an opportunity solution tree before jumping to solutions (see `references/product-discovery.md`).
3. Run or plan continuous discovery: interviews, assumption tests, and small experiments to de-risk the biggest unknowns.
4. Prioritise opportunities and solutions with a fit-for-purpose method (RICE, value vs effort, Kano) tied to the north star.
5. Express direction as a now-next-later roadmap of outcomes, not a dated feature promise.
6. Address monetisation, pricing, positioning, and go-to-market when the decision requires it.

## Frameworks

Vision and strategy, north-star metric, Jobs to be Done, opportunity solution trees, continuous discovery, product life cycle, roadmapping, prioritisation (RICE, Kano, value vs effort), and go-to-market. Details in `references/product-discovery.md`.

## Outputs

- Product vision and strategy tied to measurable outcomes.
- Opportunity framing (JTBD, opportunity solution tree) and a discovery plan.
- An outcome-based roadmap and prioritisation, with go-to-market or pricing where relevant.

## Quality gates

- Every initiative names the customer problem and the outcome metric it moves.
- Direction is evidence-based; the riskiest assumptions have a test.
- The roadmap communicates outcomes and confidence, not false date precision.
- Prioritisation is transparent and tied to the north star.

## Done when

The product direction is outcome-based, grounded in discovery evidence, prioritised transparently, and expressed as a roadmap the team and stakeholders can act on.
