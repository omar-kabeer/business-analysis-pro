# UX Research and Design Skill

## Role

Bring the user's perspective into analysis and delivery. Produce personas, journeys, and usability and accessibility guidance that keep solutions grounded in real needs and inclusive by default. This skill guides UX thinking for BAs and product roles; it does not replace a specialist design team for high-fidelity visual design.

## Inputs

- User research, interviews, support data, analytics, and any existing personas or journeys.
- The product or process context and the decision the UX work supports.
- Accessibility and compliance requirements.

## Workflow

1. Ground in evidence: gather what is known about users, tasks, and context. Flag where research is thin (hand to elicitation if needed).
2. Model the users: personas built from real segments and behaviours, and empathy maps for the target persona.
3. Map the experience: a current-state journey map with stages, actions, thoughts, emotions, pain points, and opportunities. Use a service blueprint when front stage and back stage interactions matter.
4. Assess usability against recognised heuristics (Nielsen) and flag friction, error-prone steps, and cognitive load.
5. Check accessibility against WCAG 2.1 AA: perceivable, operable, understandable, robust. Call out specific failures and fixes.
6. Give information architecture and wireframe guidance: structure, navigation, hierarchy, and key screen intents, expressed so requirements and design can execute. See `references/ux-artifacts.md` for artifact structures.

## Outputs

- Evidence-based personas and empathy maps.
- Journey map or service blueprint with pain points and opportunities.
- Usability findings, an accessibility (WCAG) assessment, and IA or wireframe guidance.

## Quality gates

- Personas and journeys come from evidence, not invention; assumptions are labelled.
- Usability findings are specific and actionable, tied to a heuristic and a fix.
- Accessibility is assessed against WCAG criteria, not vibes.
- Recommendations connect to the decision and hand cleanly to requirements.

## Done when

Users are modelled from evidence, the experience is mapped with pain points and opportunities, and usability and accessibility guidance is specific enough to inform requirements and design.
