# Communication Formats

## Tailoring by audience

- Executives and board: the decision and the ask first, then a short why, the value, and the risks. Brief. See the executive-review skill for board-level polish.
- Delivery team: the detail, the reasoning, and the implications for their work. More depth, less framing.
- Customers and end users: the impact on them, in plain language, with what changes and when.

Same facts, different shape. Decide the audience before you write.

## Stakeholder update

- Headline: the one thing to know.
- Progress since last update.
- What is next.
- Risks, issues, and decisions needed (with any ask).
- Where to go for more.

## Status or steering committee report

- Overall status (with an honest rating and the reason for it).
- Progress against plan: scope, schedule, budget.
- Key risks and issues, with owners and actions.
- Decisions needed from the committee, stated as clear asks.
- Upcoming milestones.

A status rating must reflect reality. A green that hides a real problem costs more later and erodes trust.

## Project email

- Subject line that states the point or the ask.
- First line: the purpose and any deadline.
- Short body: context, then the specific request or information.
- Clear next step and owner.

## Change announcement

- What is changing and when.
- Why, in one or two lines.
- Who is affected and what they need to do.
- Where to get help or ask questions.

## Meeting notes

- Purpose and attendees.
- Decisions made.
- Actions with owners and due dates.
- Open questions parked for follow-up.

Circulate promptly while memory is fresh, and confirm the decisions and actions with participants.
