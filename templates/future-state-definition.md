# Future State Definition

## Purpose

Define the desired future state in enough detail to guide the change strategy and to identify the value the change will deliver. This is the working form of the BABOK Define Future State task (6.2). It sets measurable objectives, bounds the solution space, records constraints and assumptions, and states the potential value.

## Inputs

The current state assessment, the business need, organisational strategy, and stakeholder input on desired outcomes.

## 1. Business goals and objectives

State the goals (longer-term, qualitative) and refine them into SMART objectives that can be measured. Objectives make it possible to tell whether the change worked and often anchor the business case.

SMART check: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bounded.

## 2. Scope of solution space

Define which kinds of options will be considered to meet the objectives: changes to organisational structure or culture, capabilities and processes, technology and infrastructure, policies, products or services, or relationships with parties currently outside the enterprise. Bounding the solution space early prevents both tunnel vision and unbounded scope.

## 6. Potential value

Describe the value the future state is expected to deliver, both tangible (measurable, often monetary) and intangible (reputation, morale, optionality), and note where value is relative rather than absolute.

## Review criteria

- Objectives are SMART and tied to measures and targets.
- The solution space is bounded deliberately and aligned to enterprise objectives.
- Constraints and assumptions are explicit, justified, and owned.
- Potential value is stated in terms that a business case can use.
